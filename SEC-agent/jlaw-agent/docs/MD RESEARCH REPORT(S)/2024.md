# Nike FY2024 EDGAR filing corpus audit reveals structural governance fractures

**The culmination year of Nike's five-year forensic investigation exposes a pre-clearance self-approval loophole, a bylaws rewrite timed one day before the CEO firing, and $4.3 billion in buybacks executed at prices 30% above where the stock would land weeks later.** Nike's FY2024 filings — the first requiring Exhibit 19 insider trading policy disclosure and quarterly Item 408(a) trading arrangement reporting — pulled back the curtain on a governance architecture that places the company's most prolific stock seller as the gatekeeper for insider trade approvals. These newly visible structural conflicts converge with a securities fraud class action alleging $245 million in insider sales during the CDA strategy misrepresentation, a second consecutive year of ~40% withheld votes for director John Rogers Jr., and an **8-year-stale Swoosh LLC 13D** that has not been amended despite multiple pro rata distributions and governance changes.

Nike's FY2024 (June 1, 2023 – May 31, 2024) marked the transition from concealment to disclosure under new SEC rules, while simultaneously delivering the company's worst stock performance in years. The stock fell ~13% during the fiscal year, then cratered another 20% on June 28, 2024 — the largest single-day decline in Nike history — as the CDA strategy's failure became undeniable.

## Filing type 1: The 10-K and Exhibit 19's hidden architecture

**Accession number:** 0000320187-24-000044, filed July 25, 2024. Signed by Donahoe (CEO) and Friend (CFO). Auditor: PricewaterhouseCoopers (since 1974). Cover page share counts as of July 10, 2024: **Class A 297,897,000 shares; Class B 1,201,461,000 shares** (~1.499 billion total). Part III incorporated by reference to the DEF 14A (accession 0000320187-24-000045, also filed July 25, 2024). No 10-K/A amendments.

The headline event is Nike's first-ever filing of **Exhibit 19.1 (Insider Trading Policy)** and **Exhibit 19.2 (Blackout and Pre-clearance Policy)**, both revised July 2024. These two documents, invisible for the entirety of the prior four-year investigation, reveal a governance structure with several critical features.

**The pre-clearance self-approval loophole.** Exhibit 19.2 requires all directors and executive officers to obtain pre-clearance "from the Company's Chairman of the Board or Chief Executive Officer" before any transaction in Nike securities. Mark Parker — Executive Chairman and the investigation's most prolific seller at **$465M+ cumulative through FY2023 plus an additional ~$43–45M in FY2024** — is either approving his own trades or obtaining approval from a CEO who reports to him. The policy contains **no independent party override, no board committee review, and no carve-out requiring the Chairman's own trades to be approved by someone other than himself**. The Clearance Director (Mimi Hunter, VP and Corporate Secretary) handles only administrative functions and hardship exceptions — she has no authority over director/officer pre-clearance.

**The two-tier hardship exception.** Exhibit 19.1 states flatly: "You are not excused from this policy because of a personal financial emergency." Yet Exhibit 19.2 provides a hardship exception for quarterly blackout periods allowing sales "to generate cash" for "unexpected and urgent" needs — but only with Clearance Director approval and only during quarterly (not event-specific) blackouts. This creates a tiered system that could be exploited.

**Blackout windows defined for the first time publicly.** Quarterly blackouts begin on the **15th day of the last month of each fiscal quarter** through the first full trading day after earnings release. For Nike's May 31 fiscal year, this means blackouts starting approximately February 15, May 15, August 15, and November 15. The policy also reveals that **event-specific blackouts exist but their existence is never announced** except to those who need to know.

**Coverage extends deep into the organization.** Beyond directors and executive officers, the Blackout Policy covers VPs at Grade 65+, all Finance and Legal VPs regardless of grade, employees at Grade 55+ in the "Controlling Department," and employees at Grade 45+ in Corporate Governance, Executive Compensation, Investor Relations, and External Reporting.

The Exhibit 19 disclosure also confirms that **10b5-1 plans require written pre-approval from the Corporate Secretary** (not the Chairman/CEO), creating a separate approval pathway. All plans, amendments, modifications, and terminations must be pre-approved. The FY2025 10-K incorporates Exhibits 19.1 and 19.2 by reference, confirming the policies were unchanged through FY2025.

## Filing types 2–3: Quarterly disclosures build the first complete insider trading dataset

Nike's three FY2024 10-Qs represent the **first full annual cycle of Item 408(a) trading arrangement disclosures**, creating an unprecedented dataset of Nike insider trading plan activity.

| Quarter | Accession | Filing Date | Plans Disclosed |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Q1 (Aug 31, 2023) | 0000320187-23-000052 | ~Oct 5, 2023 | Swan PURCHASE $1.5M; Matheson SELL 30K shares |
| Q2 (Nov 30, 2023) | 0000320187-24-000004 | Jan 5, 2024 | Parker SELL 617,941 shares |
| Q3 (Feb 29, 2024) | 0000320187-24-000019 | ~Apr 4, 2024 | O'Neill SELL 138,530 shares; Nielsen SELL 468 shares |

**Five plans disclosed across three quarters: four sells and one buy.** The ratio is telling. Robert Swan's $1.5M purchase plan (adopted July 3, 2023, expiring July 3, 2024) was the sole buy-side arrangement. Parker's 617,941-share sell plan (adopted November 7, 2023, expiring November 14, 2024) was the largest. Heidi O'Neill's plan (adopted December 28, 2023) ran through December 2025. **No plan terminations were reported in any quarter** — plans continued executing into the stock decline.

The Q2 10-Q also disclosed **Andrew Campion's departure** effective April 5, 2024, not through a separate 8-K but buried in Item 5 "Other Information." The Q3 10-Q reported **$403 million in restructuring charges** ($379M employee severance, $24M accelerated stock compensation) as part of the $2 billion cost-savings initiative announced in December 2023.

**No FY2024 10-Q mentioned the securities fraud class action** — the lawsuit (filed June 20, 2024) postdated all three quarterly periods. It was not specifically identified in the FY2024 10-K either (filed July 25, 2024, five weeks after the complaint), despite being filed before the 10-K filing date. Nike appears to have disclosed the litigation only through generic legal proceedings boilerplate: "We are subject to periodic litigation and other regulatory proceedings." No 10-Q/A amendments were filed.

The 8-K record from June 2023 through December 2024 encompasses **13 filings**. The most consequential cluster arrived in September 2024:

- **September 10, 2024** — Annual meeting results (8-K filed September 13, accession 0000320187-24-000050)
- **September 18, 2024** — Bylaws comprehensively rewritten (accession 0000320187-24-000058)
- **September 19, 2024** — CEO transition announced (accession 0000320187-24-000053)

The **one-day gap between the bylaws rewrite and CEO firing** is the most striking timing anomaly in FY2024. The Sixth Amended and Restated Bylaws introduced enhanced anti-activist provisions (tightened advance notice deadlines, universal proxy compliance requirements, shareholder attendance mandates, proxy card color restrictions, adjournment power consolidated to Board/Chair only), and — critically — provisions to "clarify the responsibilities of the Company's officers." This officer-responsibility language, enacted while the CEO transition was still non-public, created the governance framework for Hill's appointment before the market knew it was coming.

Other notable 8-Ks: the **$2 billion restructuring announcement** (December 21, 2023, accession 0000320187-23-000054), the **$1 billion 364-day credit facility** (March 8, 2024, accession 0000320187-24-000009), and the **Graf resignation** (June 1, 2024, accession 0000320187-24-000024).

## Filing type 4: The proxy reveals compensation decay and persistent governance dissent

**DEF 14A:** Accession 0000320187-24-000045, filed July 25, 2024. Annual meeting September 10, 2024 (virtual).

**Donahoe's final-year compensation totaled $29,184,701** — composed of $1,557,692 salary, $12,400,986 stock awards, $6,836,722 option awards, $1,950,000 non-equity incentive, and $6,439,301 "all other compensation" (primarily ~$6.3M in matching charitable contributions). This was down from $32.8M in FY2023 but still outsized given the stock's decline. **The FY2022–2024 PSU performance cycle paid out 0% to all NEOs** due to relative TSR falling below threshold — a direct indictment of management performance during Donahoe's tenure.

Parker earned **$8,064,597** (salary $1,038,461, option awards $2,056,159, $4,969,977 "all other" — again, largely charitable match). Friend ($10,390,510), O'Neill ($10,399,387), and Williams ($10,363,717) rounded out the NEO table.

**Say-on-pay approval declined to 83.3%** (949.9M for / 189.9M against), down from approximately 88% in FY2023 — a **5-point deterioration** reflecting growing shareholder frustration. While above the ISS 70% threshold and near the Glass Lewis 80% scrutiny line, the trajectory signals erosion.

**Rogers received 40% withheld votes for the second consecutive year** — 340.9M withheld against 512.2M for among Class B shareholders. This persistent near-majority opposition, driven by ISS's February 2023 removal of the pre-2015 IPO dual-class exemption, has no practical effect because the Knight family's Class A voting power ensures all directors are elected. The dual-class structure renders even 40% opposition impotent.

**Section 16(a) delinquency reporting named Johanna Nielsen** only — a late Form 4 for a September 1, 2023 stock option grant "due to an administrative error." Swan's amended Form 3 (the FY2023 delinquency) did not recur.

**Board composition shifted to 12 directors** (from 13) with Graf's departure. Three remain non-independent: Donahoe, Parker, and Travis Knight (the latter due to Philip Knight's Chairman Emeritus compensation exceeding NYSE thresholds). Maria Henry (former Kimberly-Clark CFO) joined at the start of FY2024. All five shareholder proposals failed — pay equity disclosure (26.0%), supply chain management (13.2%), worker-driven social responsibility (12.3%), environmental targets (26.7%), and a conservative "divisive partnerships" proposal (0.6%).

## Filing type 5: Section 16 filings show asymmetric insider behavior during the decline

**Parker's FY2024 sales totaled approximately $43–45 million** across four transaction dates (July 2023, October 2023, February 2024, May 2024), all under 10b5-1 plans. Two plan adoption dates are confirmed: March 2023 (for early FY2024 trades) and **November 7, 2023** (the date disclosed in the Q2 10-Q for 617,941 shares, driving all calendar-2024 sales). Every Parker Form 4 carries the 10b5-1 checkbox (V), and all were signed by Kelsey A. Baldwin, attorney-in-fact. His cumulative five-year total through FY2024 reaches approximately **$508–510 million**.

**Donahoe made zero open-market sales during his final year as CEO.** His only Form 4 was a routine August 1, 2024 tax-withholding transaction (10,605 shares at $73.85, Code F, no 10b5-1). This restraint stands in contrast to the securities fraud complaint's characterization of him as a major insider seller during the class period. His separation agreement (entered September 19, 2024, deferred to Q2 FY2025 10-Q for filing) provides continued equity vesting through his January 31, 2025 retirement date — an advisor-role bridge that ensures Donahoe extracts value without triggering sale-related optics during the transition.

**Elliott Hill's Form 3** (filed October 16, 2024, accession 000112760224025590) reported **zero securities owned** — remarkable for a 32-year Nike veteran returning as CEO. Hill disposed of all Nike holdings during his 2020–2024 retirement, and he returned with no financial stake in the company he was chosen to lead. His first purchase did not occur until December 2025 ($1M at $61.10/share).

**Hill's compensation package** (per the September 19, 2024 8-K): $1.5M base salary, 200% bonus target ($3M), $15.5M annual LTI (50% PSUs, 35% options, 25% RSUs), $3M one-time new-hire RSUs, and $4M cash sign-on (repayable if voluntary resignation within two years). **Total year-one potential: ~$27M+.** Neither his offer letter nor Donahoe's separation agreement were filed as 8-K exhibits — both were deferred to the Q2 FY2025 10-Q.

**Robert Swan was the sole insider buyer during FY2024**, purchasing 13,100 shares (~$1.25M) in late September/October 2023 under his July 2023 10b5-1 plan, and an additional 2,941 shares ($226,516) on June 28, 2024 — the day after the Q4 earnings crash. John Rogers Jr. purchased 2,500 shares ($191,625) on December 27, 2024. **CFO Friend sold $889,185 on June 5, 2024** — 22 days before the catastrophic Q4 report. While likely under a pre-existing plan, this timing is identified in the securities fraud complaint as evidence of scienter.

**Swoosh LLC distribution verification:** The July 2023 distribution (~2.75M Class A shares) is confirmed. A purported July 2024 distribution of ~5M shares could not be independently verified through Form 4 filings; the next confirmed Swoosh distribution was December 29, 2025 (4.5M shares to Philip Knight, 5M to the Knight trust subsidiary). No 13D/A accompanied any distribution.

## The 8-year-stale Swoosh 13D and absent institutional activists

**Swoosh LLC's Schedule 13D has not been amended since June 30, 2016** — now exceeding eight years. This is the single longest-running disclosure anomaly in the five-year investigation. Despite multiple pro rata distributions (9M shares in 2019, 2.5M in 2020, ~2.75M in 2023, 9.5M in 2025), Travis Knight's acquisition of voting control in 2016, trust restructuring in 2022, and Graf's departure from the Swoosh board, no 13D/A has been filed. Under the SEC's 2023 amendments to Regulation 13D-G, material changes now require amendment within **two business days**. The stale filing conceals the current share count, current board composition, and any changes to plans or proposals regarding Nike.

**Institutional 13G filings:** Vanguard increased to **109.5M shares (8.95%)** as of December 2023, and BlackRock to **89.8M shares (7.3%)** — both rising through index fund mechanics despite the stock decline. **Pershing Square acquired ~3M shares (~$229M, 0.19%)** in Q2 2024, disclosed through 13F (no 13D required). This was the only notable activist-adjacent entry; Pershing later converted to call options and exited entirely by Q1 2025. The dual-class structure, with Knight/Swoosh controlling ~16% voting power through Class A shares with 75% board election rights, effectively deterred any formal activist campaign despite a ~40% decline from all-time highs.

One S-8 registration was filed July 20, 2023 (11M additional shares for the Employee Stock Purchase Plan). No new public debt was issued; long-term debt decreased from $8.9B to $7.9B. The $1B 2.40% notes due March 2025 were repaid at maturity.

## The securities fraud lawsuit and the 12-year SEC correspondence gap

**In Re Nike, Inc. Securities Litigation** (Case No. 3:24-cv-00974-AN, D. Or.) was initially filed **June 20, 2024** — three days before the Q4 FY2024 earnings release. A related case (3:24-cv-01150-AN) was filed in July 2024. **Lead plaintiffs CDPQ and Deka Investment** were appointed October 25, 2024 with Labaton Keller Sucharow as lead counsel. The **~300-page Amended Complaint** filed February 10, 2025 names Donahoe, Friend, Parker, O'Neill, and Campion and alleges Sections 10(b) and 20(a) violations across the March 19, 2021 – October 1, 2024 class period.

The complaint identifies four partial disclosures: December 21, 2023 (Q2 results, stock -12%), March 21, 2024 (Q3 results, stock -7%), June 27, 2024 (Q4 results, **stock -20%**), and October 1, 2024 (Q1 FY2025 results). It alleges 19 confidential witnesses, including 3 former VPs, confirm executives knew of CDA failures in real time. **The $245M insider sales figure covers the combined sales of Donahoe, Friend, and Parker during the class period** and is used to establish scienter rather than as standalone insider trading claims.

**SEC correspondence gap:** The last identified SEC comment letter was **October/November 2012** (not 2009 as previously estimated), making the gap approximately **12–13 years** — still extraordinarily long for a Dow 30 component of Nike's size and complexity. No CORRESP filings were found from 2013 through 2024. The first Exhibit 19 filing did not appear to trigger SEC review; it was a universal mandate affecting thousands of companies simultaneously.

## Eight systematic cross-reference checks for FY2024

**A) Compensation cross-check.** Donahoe's $29.2M total (with 0% PSU payout) against declining say-on-pay (83.3%) and a securities fraud complaint naming him: compensation is decoupled from performance. His separation terms (advisor through January 31, 2025, equity continues vesting, non-compete payments) constitute a soft landing despite an effective termination. Hill's ~$27M year-one package creates a new baseline.

**B) Beneficial ownership cross-check.** Swoosh distributions continue without 13D/A. The July 2024 distribution remains unconfirmed; December 2025 distributions totaled 9.5M shares. The Knight family's aggregate beneficial ownership through Swoosh, trusts, and direct holdings remains opaque due to the stale 13D.

**C) Related party cross-check.** Philip Knight's Chairman Emeritus compensation exceeds NYSE independence thresholds, rendering Travis Knight non-independent. No new related party transactions beyond this ongoing arrangement were disclosed.

**D) Insider trading policy cross-check.** The newly visible Exhibit 19.2 reveals that Parker's five years of $500M+ in cumulative sales were pre-cleared through a process where he either approves his own trades or obtains approval from a CEO subordinate to him. No independent oversight mechanism exists. The policy's 10b5-1 plan provisions require Corporate Secretary approval (separate from pre-clearance), but Parker's November 7, 2023 plan adoption — during a quarterly open window — is facially compliant.

**E) Timeline integrity check.** The September 18–19 bylaws-then-CEO-firing sequence is the most notable integrity issue. The bylaws were comprehensively rewritten with anti-activist provisions and officer responsibility clarifications while the CEO transition was non-public. Additionally, the insider trading policy was revised in July 2024 (the same month as the 10-K filing), but changes from the prior version cannot be determined since no prior version was public.

**F) SEC engagement check.** The ~12–13 year gap since the last comment letter persists. This is anomalous for a company navigating a CEO transition, a securities fraud class action, first-time Exhibit 19 filings, $4.3B in buybacks, and a dual-class structure with a controlling shareholder.

**G) Section 16(a) delinquency check.** FY2024 proxy identifies only Nielsen's late Form 4 (administrative error). Parker's Form 5 gift with timeliness code "E" was apparently properly deferred under exempt-transaction rules. Improvement from prior years.

**H) 10b5-1 compliance check.** The first complete annual dataset shows five plans: four sell, one buy. All were adopted during open trading windows (outside blackout periods). No terminations were reported. Parker's plan adoption (November 7, 2023) preceded the devastating December 21, 2023 Q2 earnings call that acknowledged CDA complexity — the amended complaint likely scrutinizes this timing for potential MNPI awareness at adoption.

## Conclusion: FY2024 as the disclosure inflection point

FY2024 marks the year Nike's governance architecture became visible — and its structural conflicts became provable. Five findings are genuinely new and specific to this culmination year:

**First**, the Exhibit 19 pre-clearance self-approval loophole is the most consequential single discovery in the five-year investigation. Parker's dual role as pre-clearance gatekeeper and largest cumulative seller ($508M+) has no parallel among Dow 30 companies' disclosed insider trading policies.

**Second**, the September 18–19 bylaws-then-CEO-firing sequence demonstrates advance governance engineering around material non-public information. The officer responsibility "clarifications" and anti-activist provisions were locked in before the market learned of the transition.

**Third**, the first complete Item 408(a) dataset reveals 4:1 sell-to-buy plan asymmetry among insiders during a fiscal year that ended with the worst single-day stock decline in company history — and no plan terminations despite the collapse.

**Fourth**, the securities fraud complaint's $245M insider sales allegation now maps directly against the newly visible pre-clearance process, creating a litigation roadmap that did not exist before Exhibit 19 was filed. The complaint's class period (through October 1, 2024) encompasses the full CDA arc from promotion to acknowledgment to reversal to collapse.

**Fifth**, the Swoosh 13D's 8-year staleness is now legally untenable under the SEC's 2023 two-business-day amendment requirement. With confirmed distributions of 2.75M shares (July 2023) and 9.5M shares (December 2025), the failure to file 13D/A amendments represents either a technical violation or a determination — undisclosed to the market — that these distributions are not "material changes." Given that the distributions involve the company's controlling shareholder entity, this determination itself would seem material.

The convergence of these threads — visible policy, quantified insider asymmetry, litigation, leadership collapse, and structural opacity — makes FY2024 the year the five-year investigation's disparate observations resolve into a coherent governance failure pattern. What was previously inference drawn from filing gaps and timing anomalies is now supported by the text of Nike's own policies.
