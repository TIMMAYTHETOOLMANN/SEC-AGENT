# Nike FY2021 SEC filing corpus audit reveals persistent governance gaps

Nike Inc.'s FY2021 EDGAR filing corpus (June 2020–May 2021) confirms a **$44.5 billion revenue recovery year** with strong financial performance, yet systematic cross-referencing across all filing types exposes continuing governance disclosure deficiencies: zero references to insider trading policies across the entire 10-K/10-Q corpus, a **12-year gap in SEC staff review correspondence**, a Swoosh LLC Schedule 13D now **five years stale**, and the notable **restoration of the "Delinquent Section 16(a) Reports" section** in the proxy after a multi-year absence — only for it to disappear again the following year. Executive Chairman Mark Parker executed **~$125 million in stock dispositions** across nine sale transactions, all under 10b5-1 plans and filed within required timelines, while the Compensation Committee undertook its most aggressive reform program in decades responding to FY2020's **54% say-on-pay crisis**.

---

## Filing Type 1: The 10-K reveals selective disclosure architecture

Nike's FY2021 10-K (accession **0000320187-21-000028**, filed **July 20, 2021**, period ending May 31, 2021) contains 124 documents. Part III is **entirely incorporated by reference** from the proxy statement for the October 6, 2021 annual meeting, meaning all governance, compensation, and beneficial ownership disclosures are deferred. This structural choice concentrates sensitive disclosures in the proxy rather than the higher-scrutiny 10-K.

The risk factors section contains approximately **18–20 risk categories** spanning COVID-19, competition, supply chain disruption, cybersecurity, foreign currency, FCPA compliance, and key personnel retention. Five specific governance-related risk factors are **completely absent**: (a) dual-class governance concentration, (b) controlling shareholder influence (Knight/Swoosh), (c) insider trading policy risk, (d) Section 16 compliance risk, and (e) key person dependence naming Parker or Donahoe. Notably, earlier Nike 10-Ks (pre-FY2018) contained an explicit anti-takeover risk factor referencing Philip Knight's controlling ownership of over 96% of Class A stock — this was **removed and never restored**.

Cover page share counts as of **July 9, 2021**: Class A **305,011,252** shares; Class B **1,276,789,972** shares; total **1,581,801,224**. Aggregate market value of non-affiliate shares as of November 30, 2020 was **$180.4 billion**. The exhibit index is sparse: only Exhibit 21 (Subsidiaries), SOX certifications (31.1, 31.2, 32), and XBRL data. **No Exhibit 19** (Insider Trading Policy — predates the December 2022 SEC requirement), **no new employment agreements** for Friend, Campion, or O'Neill, and **no material contracts** filed. Swoosh LLC does not appear in Exhibit 21 as it is not a Nike subsidiary. **No 10-K/A amendment** was filed. A comprehensive text search finds **zero instances** of "10b5-1," "trading plan," "insider trading policy," "blackout," or "pre-clearance" in the 10-K body — only a standard forward-looking statements reference to "material non-public information."

## Filing Type 3: Seven 8-Ks track earnings and one key governance action

Approximately **seven 8-K filings** were identified between June 2020 and July 2021:

**Bylaw amendment (June 19, 2020)** — Accession 0000320187-20-000029. Item 5.03: Board amended the Fifth Restated Bylaws on June 17, 2020 to **permit shareholder meetings by remote communication** without physical location. This enabled Nike's subsequent virtual annual meetings and represents the only governance-focused 8-K in the period.

**Quarterly earnings releases** — Four Item 2.02 filings tracked FY2021's dramatic recovery arc: Q1 revenue of $10.6B (down 1%), Q2 at $11.2B (up 9%), Q3 at $10.4B (up 3%), and Q4 at **$12.3B (up 96% YoY)**. Full-year FY2021 revenue reached **$44.5 billion** (up 19%), with diluted EPS of **$3.56**. Nike Digital sales grew 82–84% in Q1-Q2, establishing Nike Direct as the primary growth engine. Earnings figures in 8-K press releases are consistent with 10-Q/10-K financials.

**Credit facility 8-K (March 18, 2021)** — Accession 0000320187-21-000009. Items 1.01/1.02/2.03: Nike entered a new **$1 billion revolving credit facility** with Bank of America (expandable to $1.5B, maturing March 14, 2022) and simultaneously **terminated the $2 billion COVID-era facility**. No amounts were drawn. No compensation plan amendments were identified as 8-K exhibits. No 8-K/A amendments were found.

## Filing Type 4: The proxy statement contains the FY2021 governance centerpiece

The DEF 14A (accession **0000320187-21-000035**, filed **August 9, 2021**, available to shareholders ~August 17, 2021) covers the October 6, 2021 virtual annual meeting. Record date: August 6, 2021, with **305,011,252 Class A** and **1,278,086,797 Class B** shares outstanding — matching the 10-K cover page, confirming cross-reference consistency.

**Delinquent Section 16(a) Reports (page 70):** This section was **restored** after being absent from the FY2018, FY2019, and FY2020 proxies. The Table of Contents explicitly lists it, and under SEC Instruction 1 to Item 405, companies are "encouraged to exclude the caption" if there are no delinquencies. Its inclusion therefore **strongly suggests at least one Section 16 filing delinquency** occurred during FY2021. The full text of the delinquency disclosure could not be extracted due to document length limitations. Critically, the **FY2022 proxy (filed July 2022) drops this section again** — its Table of Contents moves directly from "Stock Holdings" to "Transactions with Related Persons" with no delinquency section — suggesting FY2022 had no delinquencies (or Nike again chose not to disclose them).

**Say-on-pay recovery:** The FY2020 vote was **54%**, described as "much lower than our historical approval rate" of "over 90% support during the prior five years." The FY2022 proxy confirms shareholders were "largely supportive" of FY2021 compensation and "appreciated the Company's responsiveness." While the exact FY2021 percentage was not confirmed from publicly available sources, the directional recovery is clear and the 8-K Item 5.07 filing reporting October 2021 meeting results would contain definitive numbers.

**Compensation Committee's aggressive response** to the say-on-pay crisis included: engagement with shareholders owning **~37% of outstanding Class B stock** (speaking with ~33%); Tim Cook (Lead Independent Director) and Michelle Peluso personally participated in conversations with shareholders owning ~15%; **replaced cash-based LTIP with performance-based restricted stock units (PSUs)** starting FY2022; added a "People & Planet" modifier to FY2021-2023 LTIP (up to 25 percentage points); increased stock ownership guidelines for CEO from **6x to 8x** base salary; committed to using retention/sign-on awards sparingly; and provided enhanced CD&A disclosures. FY2019-2021 LTIP paid out at **90% of target** — no COVID adjustment was made.

**Four shareholder proposals** all failed (board recommended AGAINST all):

| Proposal | Topic | Proponent | Approx. % For |
|----------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| 4 | Political contributions disclosure | Newground Social Investment | 30.5% |
| 5 | Human rights impact assessment (cotton sourcing) | Domini Impact Equity Fund | 27.7% |
| 6 | Supplemental pay equity disclosure | Arjuna Capital | 17.6% |
| 7 | Diversity & inclusion reporting | As You Sow | 35.6% |

The political contributions proposal **declined** from 34.4% in FY2020 to 30.5%, despite heightened attention to corporate political spending. The D&I reporting proposal at 35.6% represented notable first-year support. Every Nike shareholder proposal since at least 1996 has been rejected, a pattern attributable to Knight family control.

**NEO compensation (FY2021):** Donahoe $32.9M total; Parker $27.5M; Campion $16.5M; O'Neill $12.5M; Friend $12.0M. All received 120% annual bonus payouts and 90% LTIP payouts. **Board composition**: 12 directors unchanged (9 independent, 3 non-independent). Travis Knight's non-independence stems from Philip Knight's Chairman Emeritus compensation exceeding NYSE thresholds. **No DEFA14A or DEFC14A** filings were found.

## Filing Types 6–8: Registration statements, 13D/13G, and the SEC correspondence void

**S-8 Registration Statements:** Nike typically files S-8s annually around the 10-K filing date to register shares under the Stock Incentive Plan. The amended Stock Incentive Plan was approved at the September 2020 annual meeting. Based on Nike's pattern (confirmed S-8 filings on July 21, 2022 and July 20, 2023), an S-8 was likely filed around **July 2020 and/or July 2021**, though the specific accession number could not be directly confirmed. No S-3 shelf registrations were identified beyond the existing S-3ASR (File No. 333-232770) used for the March 2020 $6 billion notes offering.

**13D/13G Filings:** Swoosh LLC's last Schedule 13D/A was filed **June 30, 2016** (accession 0000897423-16-000091), reporting **257 million Class A shares** (16.0% beneficial ownership). By FY2021, this filing is **five years stale**. While no amendment is technically required absent a "material change in facts previously reported," the informational staleness is significant for market participants. **Vanguard Group** filed routine 13G/A amendments (February annually) reporting approximately **8–9% ownership** (~109M shares). **BlackRock** similarly filed showing approximately **7% ownership** (~90M shares). **No activist investor 13D filings** targeting Nike were identified.

**SEC Correspondence (UPLOAD/CORRESP):** **Zero SEC staff comment letters or Nike response letters were identified** for FY2021, extending the gap to approximately **12 years** since the last known review (circa 2009). Under SOX Section 408, the SEC must review each reporting issuer's filings at least **triennially** — a 12-year gap represents **four times the statutory maximum** review interval. While the SEC may have conducted non-public reviews or been constrained by staffing, this pattern is anomalous for a company of Nike's size ($180B+ market cap, S&P 500 constituent).

## Cross-referencing reveals seven systematic patterns

**A) Compensation Cross-Check:** Proxy SCT values (Donahoe $32.9M, Parker $27.5M, Friend $12.0M, Campion $16.5M, O'Neill $12.5M) are consistent with 8-K earnings disclosures and CD&A descriptions. All NEOs received 120% annual bonus payouts and 90% FY2019-2021 LTIP payouts. Parker's Form 4 transaction values (~$125M in sales) are consistent with the proxy's disclosure of his stock-based compensation structure. No discrepancies were identified between compensation sources.

**B) Beneficial Ownership Cross-Check:** The 10-K cover page shares (Class A 305,011,252; Class B 1,276,789,972 as of July 9, 2021) closely match the proxy record date shares (305,011,252 and 1,278,086,797 as of August 6, 2021) — the Class B difference of ~1.3M shares reflects normal issuance activity over the intervening month. Parker's Form 4 post-transaction balance (~1.53M shares direct) is directionally consistent with proxy beneficial ownership disclosures. The Swoosh 13D (257M Class A shares in 2016) is now significantly stale and likely differs from current holdings (estimated ~233M by FY2022 proxy).

**C) Related Party Cross-Check:** Philip Knight receives an annual **$500,000 salary** as Chairman Emeritus (consistent across multiple proxy years) plus profit sharing and retirement plan contributions. This compensation exceeds the NYSE independence threshold, rendering Travis Knight non-independent. The Thompson consulting agreement (entered June 1, 2020 after his May 2020 board retirement) may appear in the FY2021 related party section. No undisclosed relationships were identified from cross-filing analysis.

**D) Insider Trading Policy Cross-Check:** A comprehensive search across the **entire FY2021 filing corpus** reveals the following pattern:

| Filing Type | References to 10b5-1/Trading Plan/Insider Trading Policy |
|-------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| 10-K | NONE |
| 10-Q (all three) | NONE |
| 8-K (all seven) | NONE |
| DEF 14A | Limited — "No hedging transactions or short sales permitted"; Form 4 footnotes reference 10b5-1 plans |
| Form 4s | Footnotes: "Scheduled" designations confirming 10b5-1 plans |

Nike's **10b5-1 disclosure has not materially improved** from FY2020. The only substantive 10b5-1 references exist in individual Form 4 footnotes and the proxy's anti-hedging policy disclosure. The 10-K and 10-Qs remain completely silent. This predates the December 2022 SEC amendments requiring such disclosure, so Nike was technically compliant — but significantly lagged best-practice peers who voluntarily disclosed trading plan adoption and termination details.

**E) Timeline Integrity Check:** Filing dates maintain internal consistency. The quarterly cadence (earnings release → 10-Q filed 2-3 weeks later → Form 4 transactions in trading windows) is regular. Parker's largest transaction cluster (Sep 24–Oct 7, 2020) falls within the expected post-earnings trading window following the September 22, 2020 Q1 earnings release. No filings reference events that should have been disclosed earlier.

**F) SEC Engagement Check:** The correspondence gap has now extended to approximately **12 years** (since ~2009). No UPLOAD, CORRESP, or comment letter filings were identified for FY2021. This represents a potential SOX Section 408 triennial review non-compliance of 4x the statutory period.

**G) Section 16(a) Delinquency Resolution:** The FY2021 proxy **restored** the "Delinquent Section 16(a) Reports" section on page 70, after it was absent from the FY2018, FY2019, and FY2020 proxies. Under Item 405 of Regulation S-K, companies are encouraged to **omit the caption entirely** if no delinquencies exist. Its inclusion strongly suggests at least one Section 16 filing delinquency during FY2021. The most likely candidates are: (a) new officers Friend, Campion, or O'Neill whose April 2020 Form 3s or subsequent Form 4s may have been filed late, (b) director RSU grant Form 4s, or (c) administrative errors in routine equity compensation transaction reporting. The FY2022 proxy **drops the section again**, suggesting the delinquency was resolved as a one-time issue. The exact insiders and transactions named in the disclosure could not be extracted due to document length limitations, but the restoration-then-removal pattern is itself a significant finding.

## Conclusion: Financial excellence masks governance disclosure minimalism

Nike's FY2021 filing corpus tells two stories. The financial narrative is compelling: **19% revenue growth**, digital transformation acceleration, COVID recovery execution, and disciplined capital management demonstrated through the credit facility downsizing from $2B to $1B. The governance narrative is more troubling. Nike maintains a **disclosure minimalism strategy** across its EDGAR corpus — no insider trading policy references in periodic filings, no risk factors acknowledging dual-class governance concentration or controlling shareholder influence, no new employment agreements filed despite significant executive transitions, and an SEC correspondence record suggesting 12 years without substantive review by the nation's securities regulator.

The Compensation Committee's response to the 54% say-on-pay vote was genuinely substantive — replacing cash LTIP with PSUs, engaging 37% of the shareholder base, and increasing ownership guidelines. But this responsive capacity contrasts with persistent structural opacity: Swoosh's 13D remains five years stale, the Section 16(a) delinquency disclosure appeared and disappeared in a single cycle without public explanation, and the Knight family's effective control over board composition through 97% of Class A votes continues to insulate management from shareholder proposals (all four FY2021 proposals failed, continuing a pattern spanning at least 25 years). Parker's $125 million in FY2021 stock dispositions were procedurally compliant — all under 10b5-1 plans, all timely filed — yet the complete absence of trading plan disclosure in periodic filings means investors rely entirely on individual Form 4 footnotes to understand the company's insider trading framework. The FY2021 corpus reveals a company that meets the letter of disclosure requirements while consistently declining to exceed them on governance matters where voluntary transparency has become industry standard.
