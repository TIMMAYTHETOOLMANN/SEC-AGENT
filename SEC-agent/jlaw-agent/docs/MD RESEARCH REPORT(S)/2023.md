# Nike FY2023 SEC EDGAR filing corpus audit

**Nike's fiscal year 2023 marks a regulatory inflection point where the SEC's modernized insider trading rules first touched the company's disclosure framework — yet the audit reveals Nike navigated this transition by doing the minimum required, when required, and not a day sooner.** Across every filing type on EDGAR for CIK 0000320187 during the June 2022–October 2023 period, a consistent pattern emerges: formal compliance was met at each legal deadline, but voluntary transparency on governance-sensitive topics — 10b5-1 plans, Swoosh LLC control, dual-class risks — remained absent for a fifth consecutive year. The FY2023 proxy restored the previously dropped Section 16(a) delinquency section (disclosing one late filing by new director Robert Swan), and the mandatory Form 4 checkbox confirmed for the first time in machine-readable format that Mark Parker's sales were 10b5-1 transactions. Meanwhile, the inventory crisis that dominated FY2023 operations produced $9.7 billion in peak inventory, **330 basis points of gross margin compression**, and quiet wholesale relationship restoration — none of which was framed as strategic failure in any filing.

## The 10b5-1 compliance transition revealed in real time

The SEC's December 14, 2022 final rules created three new disclosure mechanisms, each with different effective dates for Nike. The audit confirms Nike adopted each at the precise compliance deadline — never earlier.

**Form 4 mandatory checkbox (effective April 1, 2023).** The most consequential FY2023 change occurred at the Form 4 level. Pre-April 2023 filings used EDGAR schema X0306 with **no `<aff10b5One>` XML element** — 10b5-1 references appeared only in optional footnotes (e.g., "Sale was pursuant to an approved 10b5-1 trading plan"). Post-April 2023 filings used schema X0508 with the mandatory checkbox. Parker's **April 6, 2023 Form 4** (accession 0001127602-23-012772) was the first Nike filing to include `<aff10b5One>1</aff10b5One>`, confirming the transaction was conducted under a Rule 10b5-1 plan. Every subsequent Parker sale through October 2023 carried the same flag. This is the first time Parker's 10b5-1 usage was confirmed through a standardized, machine-readable mechanism rather than voluntary footnotes — significant given his cumulative sales exceeding **$420 million** across prior years with no proxy disclosure of plan existence.

**Exhibit 19 — Insider Trading Policy (not yet required for FY2023).** The task's premise that Exhibit 19 was required for FY2023 requires correction. The SEC rule mandates Exhibit 19 filing for the first annual report covering a fiscal year *beginning* on or after April 1, 2023 — not *ending* after that date. Nike's FY2023 began June 1, 2022, before the threshold. Nike correctly first filed Exhibit 19 with its **FY2024 10-K** (accession 0000320187-24-000044, filed July 25, 2024), disclosing two documents: Exhibit 19.1 (Insider Trading Policy) covering MNPI prohibitions, tipping rules, and covered persons; and Exhibit 19.2 (Blackout and Pre-clearance Policy) detailing quarterly blackout windows, mandatory pre-clearance for directors and officers at VP Grade 65+, and hardship exception procedures through the Corporate Secretary's office.

**Quarterly 10-Q trading arrangement disclosures (not yet required for FY2023 10-Qs).** The first Nike 10-Q to include Item 408(a) disclosures was the **Q1 FY2024 filing** (accession 0000320187-23-000052, period ending August 31, 2023), which disclosed two arrangements: director Robert Swan adopted a 10b5-1 plan on July 3, 2023 to purchase up to $1.5 million in shares (expiring July 3, 2024), and EVP Monique Matheson adopted a plan on August 1, 2023 to sell up to 30,000 shares (expiring August 1, 2024). No FY2023 10-Q contained such disclosures.

**Proxy Item 408(b) disclosure (not required until FY2024 proxy).** The FY2023 proxy contains **zero 10b5-1 disclosure** — no insider trading policy section, no plan adoption/termination narrative, no voluntary early compliance. This marks the **fifth consecutive year** (FY2019–FY2023) with no proxy-level 10b5-1 transparency.

## Section 16(a) delinquency restored after one-year absence

The FY2023 proxy (page 67) **restores the "Delinquent Section 16(a) Reports" section** that was conspicuously dropped from the FY2022 proxy. The exact text states: *"Based solely on a review of such reports furnished to the Company and written representations that no other reports were required, during fiscal 2023, we believe that all such reports that were required to be filed under Section 16(a) were timely filed, **except that an amended Form 3 was filed for Robert Swan on June 22, 2023 to report certain indirect holdings that were inadvertently omitted from his initial, timely Form 3.**"*

The single delinquency — Swan's amended Form 3 correcting omitted indirect holdings — is administrative rather than transactional. Swan was appointed September 20, 2022 and filed his initial Form 3 timely on September 21, 2022 (accession 0001127602-22-022791), but the correction required an amendment eight months later. The FY2024 proxy disclosed a separate delinquency: a late Form 4 for Johanna Nielsen's September 1, 2023 stock option grant, also attributed to administrative error. The restoration of this section after its FY2022 disappearance likely reflects improved governance practices or legal counsel guidance rather than a deterioration in filing discipline.

## Swoosh LLC at seven years of 13D staleness

The Swoosh LLC Schedule 13D remains unamended since **July 1, 2016** (Amendment No. 2), now **seven years stale** as of FY2023. The last amendment reported 257 million Class A shares representing ~16% beneficial ownership, with Travis Knight appointed President, Secretary, Treasurer, and controlling both Class X Board Seats. Despite Travis Knight's trust restructuring on **March 31, 2022** — which potentially altered beneficial ownership or control arrangements within Swoosh — no 13D/A was filed. Section 13(d) requires prompt amendment for material changes in the information disclosed.

A new data point emerged during FY2023: Swoosh LLC filed a **Form 4 on July 14, 2023** (transaction date July 12, 2023) disclosing a pro rata distribution of **2,750,000 Class A shares** to its members, reducing holdings from 233.5 million to **230,750,000 shares**. The footnote states: "Private Pro rata distribution to members of Swoosh, LLC." Signed by Raymond Durham, Vice President of Swoosh LLC. Similar distributions occurred in July 2024 (5 million shares) and December 2025 (5 million shares), indicating a **routine annual estate planning distribution pattern**. While these distributions are reported on Form 4 (as required for a 10% holder), they do not trigger 13D amendment requirements unless material changes in control or purpose occur — though the cumulative reduction from 257 million to 230.75 million shares (a **10.2% decrease**) without any 13D/A filing raises questions about the adequacy of the original disclosure.

The FY2023 proxy describes Swoosh in Travis Knight's biography — noting he "has a significant role in the management of the Class A Stock owned by Swoosh, LLC" — and in the Capital Structure section. **Parker's biography contains no mention of Swoosh** despite his role as a Swoosh LLC board member, and the proxy's independence analysis addresses Knight's non-independence through Philip Knight's Chairman Emeritus compensation rather than the Swoosh governance structure directly.

## The inventory crisis across filing types

Nike's FY2023 inventory trajectory represents the dominant operational narrative across all filing types:

| Period | Inventory | YoY Change | Gross Margin Impact |
|--------|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| Q1 FY2023 (Aug 31, 2022) | **$9,662M** | +44% | Declining |
| Q2 FY2023 (Nov 30, 2022) | $9,326M | +43% | Declining |
| Q3 FY2023 (Feb 28, 2023) | $8,900M | +16% | **-330 bps to 43.3%** |
| Q4 FY2023 (May 31, 2023) | $8,454M | Flat | -140 bps to 43.6% |

The true peak was **$9,662 million in Q1** (not the commonly cited $9.3B figure from Q2 earnings), with North America inventory up 65% year-over-year. All three 10-Qs described inventory increases as driven by "elevated in-transit inventories from ongoing supply chain volatility" and "higher product input costs." The Q3 10-Q explicitly noted "higher markdowns to liquidate inventory" as the primary driver of margin compression, and management stated inventory levels would "normalize by the end of fiscal 2023." Despite this being one of the most significant inventory management crises in Nike's history, **no standalone inventory management risk factor** appeared in the FY2023 10-K's Item 1A — the risk was addressed only indirectly through macroeconomic risk language about "increased inventories" as a consequence of reduced consumer spending.

Throughout FY2023, Nike repurchased **$5.5 billion in stock** (50 million shares) under the $18 billion program approved June 2022. The Q2 FY2023 average repurchase price was $99.28 per share — meaningfully below the June 2022 starting price of ~$117 — meaning the company was buying back shares during the same period it was aggressively marking down inventory.

## Board governance, compensation, and the annual meeting

The board expanded from 12 to **13 directors** during FY2023 with three additions: Mónica Gil (NBCUniversal Telemundo, appointed September 20, 2022, Compensation Committee), Robert Swan (former Intel CEO, appointed September 20, 2022, Audit & Finance Committee), and Maria Henry (former Kimberly-Clark CFO, appointed June 1, 2023, Audit & Finance Committee). Elizabeth Comstock retired at the September 2022 meeting. Timothy Cook continued as Lead Independent Director. **Alan Graf Jr. remained Audit & Finance Committee Chair** with 21 years of board service at age 69 — approaching Nike's mandatory retirement age of 72 for directors first elected after fiscal 1993. Graf subsequently announced his retirement in FY2024, with Swan succeeding him as Audit Chair effective August 1, 2024.

The September 12, 2023 annual meeting (8-K accession 0000320187-23-000045) confirmed Swoosh LLC's unilateral Class A control: all nine Class A directors received exactly **304,257,348 votes for, zero withheld** — Swoosh's block vote. Among Class B directors, **John Rogers Jr. received 334,402,709 withheld votes (35.5%)**, by far the highest opposition — likely reflecting ISS/Glass Lewis recommendations against directors at dual-class companies. ISS's February 2023 policy update removed the pre-2015 IPO exemption, directly targeting Nike's structure. Say-on-pay recovered to **~88% approval** (1,089,327,174 for / 148,866,857 against), a significant rebound from FY2022's 65%. Two shareholder proposals (pay equity from Arjuna Capital, supply chain from Tulipshare) both failed.

Donahoe's Annual Direct Compensation was **$25,537,826** (including $1.5M salary, $3.57M PSP, $10.25M PSUs, $2.97M RSUs, $7.25M stock options); his Summary Compensation Table total was approximately **$32.8 million** including LTIP payout. The May 24, 2023 8-K (accession 0000320187-23-000020) disclosed a significant leadership restructuring effective June 1, 2023: Andrew Campion ceased serving as COO (moved to Managing Director, Strategic Business Ventures — effectively a demotion), Matthew Friend absorbed operational responsibilities including procurement and demand/supply management into his expanded CFO role, and Heidi O'Neill's title expanded to President, Consumer, Product & Brand. The COO elimination and operational function redistribution occurred without any 8-K framing as restructuring.

## What Nike's FY2023 filings did not say

The most revealing dimension of this audit is what FY2023 filings systematically omitted:

**No dual-class governance risk factor.** For the fifth consecutive year, Nike's 10-K Item 1A contains no standalone risk factor addressing the dual-class share structure, Knight family control, or governance concentration. ISS and Glass Lewis both flagged Nike's structure in 2023 policy updates, and Rogers' 35.5% withheld votes signal growing institutional discontent — yet the 10-K is silent.

**No CDA strategy reversal disclosure.** During FY2023, Nike quietly began restoring wholesale relationships with Foot Locker (announced March 2023), Macy's (June 2023), and DSW (June 2023) after years of aggressive direct-to-consumer pivot under the Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy. On earnings calls, management framed wholesale growth as "complementary" rather than corrective. CFO Friend acknowledged on Q2 FY2023: "We've been starving the wholesale channel for six to eight quarters." The actual admission of CDA's shortcomings came nine months later on the Q3 FY2024 call (March 2024). This strategic reversal — now the centerpiece of the securities fraud class action — was not disclosed as a new risk factor.

**No securities fraud litigation disclosure.** The class action (In Re Nike, D. Or. 3:24-cv-00974-AN) was filed July 2024, after FY2023 filings. However, the class period (March 19, 2021 – October 1, 2024) encompasses the entirety of FY2023. The First Amended Complaint names Donahoe, Friend, Parker, O'Neill, and Campion, alleging materially false statements about CDA's success and **$245 million in insider sales** at artificially inflated prices. Lead plaintiffs are CDPQ (Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec) and Deka Investment GmbH. FY2023 legal proceedings disclosures contain only boilerplate language.

**Zero governance topics on earnings calls.** Across all four FY2023 quarterly calls, management never discussed 10b5-1 compliance, insider trading policy changes, Swoosh LLC, the dual-class structure, or any governance reform. Calls focused exclusively on inventory normalization, margins, China recovery, and currency headwinds.

## Conclusion

FY2023 represents a year of **minimal-compliance transition** rather than genuine transparency reform. Nike met every new SEC requirement precisely at its legal deadline — the Form 4 checkbox appeared April 6, 2023; the 10-Q trading arrangement disclosures appeared in Q1 FY2024; Exhibit 19 appeared in the FY2024 10-K — but made no voluntary early adoption and no supplementary disclosure. The proxy's restoration of the Section 16(a) delinquency section (reporting Swan's single amended Form 3) is a modest governance improvement that simultaneously highlights the FY2022 removal's anomalous nature. Parker's selling continued at ~$44 million over seven months with the new 10b5-1 checkbox now formally confirming what footnotes previously indicated, though plan adoption dates remained undisclosed. The Swoosh LLC 13D entered its seventh year of staleness, the SEC comment letter gap extended to approximately a decade, and the triple-insider Executive Committee (Parker/Donahoe/Knight) continued to exercise board-level emergency authority without formal meetings. The inventory crisis produced the most operationally significant disclosures of any FY2023 filing — $9.7 billion peak, 330 basis points of margin destruction, $5.5 billion in concurrent buybacks — while the strategic reversal underlying that crisis received no candid disclosure until well into FY2024. ISS's removal of the pre-2015 IPO exemption for dual-class structures created a new external governance pressure point that manifested in Rogers' unprecedented 35.5% withheld votes but produced no disclosed board-level response. The securities fraud complaint's characterization of FY2023 as a period when executives "knew the CDA strategy was failing" while making "optimistic statements that inflated the stock price" will ultimately test whether Nike's FY2023 disclosure framework was adequate — a question the filings themselves conspicuously avoid raising.
