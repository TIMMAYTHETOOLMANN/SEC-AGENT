# Nike FY2025 SEC filing corpus audit reveals deepening governance gaps

**Nike's first full fiscal year under CEO Elliott Hill produced an operationally transformed company that left its core governance architecture entirely intact.** Despite a 10% revenue decline to $46.3 billion, a pending securities fraud class action naming five former and current executives, dual credit rating downgrades, and the largest-ever Swoosh LLC share distribution, the Exhibit 19 insider trading policy self-approval loophole remains unaddressed, the Swoosh 13D is now **9+ years stale**, and SEC correspondence silence has stretched to **~13 years**. The most striking FY2025-specific anomaly is a stark buy/sell divergence: directors and the new CEO collectively purchased **~$6.2 million** in open-market shares at multi-year lows while officers — led by Parker's **~$57.5 million** in continued 10b5-1 sales — maintained the sell-dominant pattern that spans this entire six-year investigation. Hill's turnaround restructured leadership more aggressively than any period in Nike history but did not touch governance, insider trading policies, or the Knight-family control architecture.

## Exhibit 19 unchanged and the self-approval pathway persists

The single most consequential governance finding of this audit is that **Exhibit 19 was not refiled** with the FY2025 10-K. Both Exhibit 19.1 (Insider Trading Policy) and Exhibit 19.2 (Blackout and Pre-clearance Policy) are incorporated by reference from the FY2024 10-K filed July 25, 2024. The policy text governing who pre-clears insider trades — the document that revealed Parker's ability to effectively approve his own transactions through the Chairman/CEO self-approval pathway — **remains identical to the version first disclosed 12 months earlier**.

Item 408(b), the mandatory annual narrative about the adequacy of insider trading policies, was deferred entirely to the proxy statement rather than appearing in the 10-K itself. The 10-K states: *"The information required by Item 408(b)(1) of Regulation S-K regarding our insider trading policies is included under 'Additional Information — Insider Trading Arrangements and Policies' in the definitive Proxy Statement."* This is permissible but places the adequacy assessment in a document filed simultaneously with the 10-K rather than integrating it into the annual report's governance discussion. The proxy's Section 16(a) delinquency disclosure at page 75 is confirmed present — the heading's inclusion in the table of contents **strongly suggests at least one late filer is named**, though the specific individuals could not be extracted from available renders.

For Q4 FY2025 trading arrangement disclosures under Item 408(a), the 10-K's Item 9B states that **no directors or officers adopted, modified, or terminated** any Rule 10b5-1 or non-Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangement during the quarter ending May 31, 2025. Across the full fiscal year, the Item 408(a) disclosures reveal a limited dataset: Parker's 10b5-1 plan transitioned from a November 7, 2023 plan to a **November 7, 2024** plan; Leinwand adopted a plan on November 14, 2024; and Friend's February 14, 2024 plan remained operative. Hill made no 10b5-1 plan adoptions — his December 2025 purchase was entirely discretionary.

## The insider buy/sell divergence is the defining FY2025 signal

For the first time in this six-year investigation, a cohort of insiders **bought Nike stock with personal funds** in significant volume. This occurred against the backdrop of Parker's unbroken selling cadence and represents the starkest behavioral divergence in the audit's history.

## Swoosh's 9-year 13D silence and the December 2025 mega-distribution

The Swoosh LLC Schedule 13D has not been amended since **June 30, 2016** — now a **9-year, 8-month gap** and counting. During this period, Swoosh has executed at least three major share distributions (2.75 million shares in July 2023, ~5 million in July 2024, and **9.5 million in December 2025**), the triple-insider board lost two of its three "Independent Directors" (Donahoe in October 2024 and Graf in June 2024), and the SEC's 2023 amendments to Rule 13d-2 shortened the amendment deadline to **two business days** for material changes (effective February 5, 2024). No 13D/A has appeared on EDGAR.

The December 29, 2025 distribution was the **largest in Swoosh's history**: 5 million Class A shares distributed pro rata to members, reducing Swoosh's direct holdings to **221,750,000 shares**. Phil Knight received 4.5 million shares personally, then converted all 4.5 million from Class A to Class B on February 2, 2026, and gifted 4.5 million Class B shares to his spouse on February 11, 2026. Travis Knight's entities received 5 million shares through trust structures, with 3 million distributed from the Travis A. Knight 2009 Irrevocable Trust II and contributed to the Three Strings partnership. **Travis Knight's Form 4 for the December 22, 2025 transaction was not filed until January 5, 2026 — approximately 10 business days late**, significantly exceeding the two-business-day requirement.

The Swoosh board composition remains **completely opaque**. The last 13D described a five-seat board: two Class X seats (controlled by a Travis Knight trust subsidiary) and three Independent Director seats. Parker, Donahoe, and Graf held the Independent Director seats from January 2020. With Donahoe and Graf both departed from Nike and presumably from Swoosh, at least two seats are either vacant or filled by unknown individuals. No public filing discloses who currently governs the entity controlling Nike's super-voting Class A shares. The 10-K cover page confirms **288,887,752 Class A shares outstanding** (down ~9 million from FY2024's 297.9 million), while Class B shares fell to **1,188,015,740** (reflecting $3 billion in buybacks retiring 37.6 million shares).

Parker's biography in the DEF 14A contains **no mention of Swoosh LLC**. Swoosh is referenced only in the Capital Structure section as the entity formed by Philip Knight in 2015. The Class A director elections at the September 9, 2025 annual meeting produced a telling result: all nine Class A directors — including Parker — received **288,247,848 votes FOR with zero withheld**, confirming unanimous Swoosh block-voting. By contrast, Class B director Rogers continued receiving approximately **40% withheld votes**, perpetuating the ISS-driven opposition pattern that began when ISS removed its pre-2015 dual-class exemption in February 2023.

## Securities fraud litigation reaches the motion-to-dismiss inflection point

The *In Re Nike, Inc. Securities Litigation* (D. Or. 3:24-cv-00974-AN) progressed through critical milestones in FY2025. CDPQ and Deka were appointed lead plaintiffs on October 25, 2024. The **~292-page First Amended Consolidated Class Action Complaint** was filed February 10, 2025, expanding the class period to March 19, 2021 through October 1, 2024, adding Parker as a named defendant alongside Donahoe, Friend, O'Neill, and Campion, and alleging **$245 million in insider sales** (1.95 million shares) by Donahoe, Friend, and Parker while the stock was allegedly artificially inflated. The complaint deploys **19 confidential witnesses**, including three former VPs, describing "severe, contemporaneous problems in all six CDA components."

Defendants filed a motion to dismiss (estimated June–July 2025), and **plaintiffs filed their opposition on August 11, 2025**, arguing that the confidential witnesses' accounts are "further corroborated by defendants' later admissions and news reports — undermining their positive public statements when made." As of March 2026, **the motion to dismiss remains pending** before Judge Adrienne Nelson. Discovery is automatically stayed under the PSLRA during this phase. No settlement discussions have been publicly reported.

Nike's disclosure of this litigation across its SEC filings is remarkably restrained. The 10-K's Item 3 (Legal Proceedings) uses only generic boilerplate language — the case is **not specifically named**. The Commitments and Contingencies notes in each 10-Q state that Nike "is unable to estimate the range of loss" and that resolution "could have a material adverse effect on the Company's results of operations." Hill **never addressed the lawsuit on any earnings call** during FY2025. Two named defendants remain in senior positions: Friend as CFO and Parker as Executive Chairman. O'Neill, also named, departed in May 2025 with her role eliminated under Hill's restructuring — her separation date is September 5, 2025.

## Financial deterioration, tariff emergence, and Hill's operational overhaul

Nike's FY2025 financial trajectory reflected both the CDA strategy's legacy damage and the deliberate pain of Hill's marketplace reset. Full-year revenue fell to **$46.3 billion** (down 10% reported, 9% currency-neutral). Gross margin compressed from **45.4%** in Q1 to **40.3%** by Q4 — a 510-basis-point swing driven by accelerating promotional activity to clear inventory, unfavorable channel mix, and tariff costs. Diluted EPS declined to **$2.16** (down 42% from $3.73). Nike returned $5.3 billion to shareholders ($3 billion in buybacks, $2.3 billion in dividends) while maintaining $9.2 billion in liquidity.

Tariffs emerged as the dominant new risk factor. The 10-K's risk factor section was **materially expanded** with language acknowledging that "changes in, and responses to, U.S. trade policies, including the imposition of tariffs or penalties on imported goods...have negatively affected, and could in the future materially negatively affect, U.S. corporations, including NIKE." Nike disclosed manufacturing concentration: Vietnam **51%** of footwear, Indonesia 28%, China 17%. The Q4 FY2025 earnings call quantified the impact at **~$1 billion annualized incremental cost**, later revised upward to **$1.5 billion** in Q1 FY2026 guidance. Nike committed to reducing China's share of U.S. footwear imports from 16% to high-single-digits by end of FY2026. Footwear prices rose **17%** and apparel **14%** between September 2024 and September 2025. Credit agencies responded with dual downgrades: **S&P from AA- to A+ (July 2025)** and **Moody's from A1 to A2 (November 2025)**.

Hill's leadership restructuring was the most aggressive in Nike history. Over eight months, he eliminated the CTO, CCO, and President of Consumer/Product/Brand roles; created a new COO position (Venkatesh Alagirisamy, effective December 8, 2025); expanded CFO Friend's mandate to include global sales and Nike Direct; appointed new chiefs of people, strategy, legal, and innovation; and elevated four regional presidents. The "Win Now" strategy — repositioning Nike Digital as a full-price platform, reinvesting in wholesale partners, and leading with sport rather than lifestyle — showed early category traction (Running grew mid-single-digits by Q3) but produced the worst full-year financial results in over a decade. Hill's PSP annual bonus paid **0%** (both revenue and EBIT below threshold), and his FY2023–2025 PSU cycle also paid **0%** (4th percentile relative TSR).

## The SEC correspondence gap widens to 13 years as governance anomalies accumulate

The last confirmed SEC comment letter to Nike dates to **October 24, 2012** — a ~13-year gap without visible regulatory review correspondence. This is extraordinary for a Dow Jones component with $46+ billion in revenue. During FY2025 alone, Nike experienced an abrupt CEO termination, comprehensive bylaws rewriting, the first-ever Exhibit 19 filing, a pending securities fraud class action, dual credit downgrades, 10% revenue decline, and the largest Swoosh share distribution in history. None of these events produced detectable SEC engagement.

The **12-member board** nominated for the September 2025 annual meeting reflects active refreshment: Hill (added October 2024) and Knudstorp (added September 2025) replaced Donahoe and Graf (both departed 2024), with Benko retiring at the 2025 meeting. Cook was reappointed Lead Independent Director in June 2025 for a new three-year term. Nine of twelve directors are independent; Parker, Hill, and Travis Knight are non-independent. Parker remains **Executive Chairman** with no announced reduction in authority, though his departure from Disney's board in January 2025 may indicate increased Nike focus. The say-on-pay trajectory continued its post-2021 decline: **~82.6%** at the September 2024 meeting (the seventh consecutive data point: 97→54→72→65→88→83→82.6). The 2025 meeting approved say-on-pay but specific tallies were not fully extracted. **Zero shareholder proposals** were submitted for the 2025 meeting — a dramatic shift from five proposals in 2024 — suggesting either activist fatigue or a calculation that Hill's appointment mooted the governance critique.

No new S-8 registration statements were filed during FY2025. The September 2025 meeting approved a **45-million-share increase** to the Stock Incentive Plan. An S-3ASR automatic shelf registration was filed July 17, 2025, replacing the expiring 2022 shelf, but remains unused. Institutional ownership showed significant movement: Pershing Square built an approximately **18.77-million-share position** by year-end 2024 (~$1.4 billion), converted entirely to deep-in-the-money call options in early Q1 2025, then **fully exited by ~Q3 2025** — citing loss of turnaround confidence, tariff exposure (~$1.5 billion annual cost), and competitive pressure from On and Hoka. Vanguard held 8.95% and BlackRock 7.3% per their most recent 13G/A filings (year-end 2023 data). No activist 13D filings targeting Nike were identified — Hill's preemptive restructuring appeared to forestall formal activist campaigns despite the stock declining approximately 35% from its 2021 highs.

## Conclusion: governance continuity amid operational revolution

The FY2025 corpus audit reveals a company undergoing the most aggressive operational transformation in its history while leaving every structural governance mechanism unchanged. Six anomalies specific to FY2025 demand attention:

**First**, the Exhibit 19 self-approval loophole persists — neither Hill nor the board used the CEO transition as an opportunity to reform pre-clearance governance, despite the pathway being publicly exposed for the first time just 12 months earlier. **Second**, the Swoosh 13D's 9+ year staleness now represents a potential violation of the SEC's 2024-effective two-business-day amendment rule following the 9.5-million-share December 2025 distribution — the largest in Swoosh history. **Third**, Travis Knight's Form 4 filing delay (~10 business days late) for a December 2025 transaction is the first confirmed Section 16 timeliness failure identified in this investigation. **Fourth**, the securities fraud motion to dismiss — pending since mid-2025 — represents an existential juncture: denial opens discovery into the 19 confidential witnesses' accounts and the $245 million insider sales pattern. **Fifth**, the buy/sell divergence is unprecedented: **$6.2 million in discretionary director/CEO purchases** versus **$57.5 million in Parker 10b5-1 sales** creates a bifurcated insider signal where the governance principals and the operational principals are making diametrically opposed market bets. **Sixth**, the complete absence of SEC correspondence across 13 years of escalating governance complexity — spanning a CEO firing, bylaws rewrite, first-ever Exhibit 19 filing, securities fraud litigation, and dual credit downgrades — raises foundational questions about regulatory engagement with the filing corpus this audit has documented.
