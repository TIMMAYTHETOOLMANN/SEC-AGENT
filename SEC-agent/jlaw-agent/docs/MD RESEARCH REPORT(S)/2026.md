# Nike SEC EDGAR filing audit: January 1–March 13, 2026

**Nike made exactly eight public filings during this 73-day window — six Form 4s, one Form 144, and one 8-K — while critical governance gaps from the seven-year investigation not only persist but have deepened.** The Swoosh LLC Schedule 13D remains unamended at 9.7 years stale despite a December 2025 mega-distribution that clearly triggered a 2-business-day filing obligation now **75+ business days overdue**. The SEC correspondence gap has stretched past 14 years with no break. The securities fraud class action's motion to dismiss sits fully briefed but unruled for approximately six months. And three C-suite officers — including CFO Matthew Friend, a named litigation defendant — quietly adopted 10b5-1 trading plans during Q2 FY2026, a sharp departure from two prior quarters of zero adoptions. Nike stock trades near $54, down more than 70% from its 2021 all-time high.

## The Knight family transaction chain and Travis Knight's late filing

Phil Knight's three-step transaction chain — receive from Swoosh, convert, gift to spouse — executed flawlessly from a filing-timeliness standpoint. The **4,500,000 Class A shares** received from Swoosh LLC on December 29, 2025 (reported on Form 4 filed December 30, 2025, accession 0000320187-25-000156) were converted 1:1 to Class B on February 2, 2026, with the Form 4 filed February 4 (within two business days). Those same shares were then gifted to Penelope P. Knight on February 11, with the Form 4 filed February 13 (also within deadline). The chain reduced Phil Knight's direct Class B holdings from 12.8 million to 8.3 million shares while parking 4.5 million in his spouse's name, with beneficial ownership disclaimed.

Travis Knight's filing tells a different and forensically significant story. His December 22, 2025 transaction — contributing **1,694,859 shares** previously received from a GRAT on October 29, 2025, to Three Strings Investors L.P., a partnership he fully controls — carried a §16(a) deadline of December 24, 2025. The Form 4 was not filed until January 5, 2026, making it approximately **8–10 business days late** even accounting for the Christmas and New Year federal holidays. This remains the **first confirmed Knight family §16 timeliness failure** in the seven-year investigation. The same Form 4 also reported a December 31 distribution of 3,000,000 shares from the 2009 Irrevocable Trust II, filed three business days after that transaction — technically one day late but within a more ambiguous zone given the New Year holiday.

## Swoosh LLC's Schedule 13D: now a decade-scale delinquency

The investigation's most consequential finding has worsened. **Swoosh LLC (CIK 0001645433) has not amended its Schedule 13D since June 2016 — a gap approaching 10 years.** The December 29, 2025 mega-distribution of approximately 9.5 million Class A shares (4.5 million to Phil Knight, the remainder to family members including Travis Knight) unambiguously constitutes a material change to Items 3, 4, and 5 of the 13D. Under the **2024-effective amendments to Rule 13d-2(a)** (SEC Release Nos. 33-11253; 34-98704, effective February 5, 2024), the amendment deadline was two business days — approximately December 31, 2025. As of March 13, 2026, the filing is **75+ business days overdue**.

The staleness extends far beyond the distribution. The last amendment in 2016 reflected the transfer of voting control from Phil Knight to a Travis Knight–controlled trust and updated Swoosh's address to Hillsboro, Oregon. It does not reflect any of the subsequent changes in Swoosh's governance, beneficial ownership structure, or the entity's current share count. Nike's EDGAR beneficial ownership page confirms: **"No beneficial ownership filings for this company in last days."** This is not a minor technical oversight. For a 5%+ holder of one of America's most widely held stocks, a near-decade of 13D silence represents a systemic failure of compliance that the SEC's Division of Enforcement could, in theory, pursue under Section 13(d)(2) of the Exchange Act.

## Securities fraud class action: six months and counting without a ruling

**In Re Nike, Inc. Securities Litigation** (D. Or. 3:24-cv-00974-AN) remains in a holding pattern. The motion to dismiss, fully briefed since approximately September–October 2025, has been under advisement by Judge Adrienne Nelson for roughly **five to six months** with no ruling as of March 13, 2026. No new docket activity has been publicly identified in the audit window. Both Labaton Keller Sucharow and Cohen Milstein still list the case as "Ongoing."

The amended complaint, filed February 10, 2025, runs **292 pages**, names five defendants (Donahoe, Friend, Parker, O'Neill, Campion), cites **19 confidential witnesses** (former Nike employees), and extends the class period from March 19, 2021 through October 1, 2024. Insider sales totaling approximately **$245 million** during the class period form a key scienter allegation. Nike argued "fraud by hindsight" and safe harbor protection for forward-looking statements. Plaintiffs countered that the confidential witnesses "detail severe, contemporaneous problems in all six CDA components," making the statements not genuinely forward-looking.

The litigation risk profile remains **moderate-to-high**. If the MTD is denied in whole or substantial part, the case — led by institutional plaintiffs CDPQ and Deka Investment GmbH with top-tier counsel — could carry settlement exposure in the **$100M–$500M+ range** based on comparable cases. Nike has not accrued any loss contingency in its SEC filings and does not name the case in its 10-Q legal proceedings disclosures — using only generic boilerplate language in Note 11 referencing "various legal proceedings."

A significant new litigation front opened on **February 4, 2026**, when the **EEOC filed a subpoena enforcement action** against Nike in the Eastern District of Missouri (Case No. 4:26-mc-00128), investigating claims of systemic DEI-related race discrimination against white employees. The investigation stems from a January 2024 complaint filed by America First Legal. Nike has resisted the EEOC subpoena as "an impermissible fishing expedition."

## Three C-suite officers adopted 10b5-1 plans in a departure from prior quarters

The Q2 FY2026 10-Q (filed December 30, 2025, accession 0000320187-25-000151, for the period ending November 30, 2025) disclosed under **Item 408(a)** that three officers adopted Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangements during the quarter: **Matthew Friend** (CFO), **Phil McCartney** (EVP, Chief Innovation Officer), and **Rob Leinwand** (EVP, Chief Legal Officer). This is a sharp break from the two preceding quarters — Q1 FY2026 and Q2 FY2025 both explicitly stated that no officers adopted or terminated trading plans.

The forensic significance is threefold. Friend is a **named defendant** in the securities fraud class action. Leinwand, as Chief Legal Officer, oversees compliance with the very insider trading policy he is now trading under — and indeed sold **9,065 shares** on February 12, 2026, at $62.33 per share, the only open-market insider sale in the audit window. And the **Exhibit 19 insider trading policy remains unchanged since FY2024**, meaning the self-approval loophole identified in July 2024 — whereby the Executive Chairman (Parker) appears to lack independent pre-clearance oversight — persists unmodified through the Q2 FY2026 filing.

**Mark Parker recorded no Form 4 activity** during the audit window. His last transaction was a November 14, 2025 sale of 86,078 shares at $64.80 ($5.6 million) under a 10b5-1 plan adopted November 7, 2024. The absence of Q1 2026 sales could indicate a plan expiration, a blackout period, or a deliberate pause — but given Parker's historical pattern of near-quarterly dispositions totaling **$565M+ cumulative**, the silence is notable.

## Financial position and the road to Q3 earnings

Nike's Q2 FY2026 results (reported December 18–19, 2025) beat consensus estimates — **$0.53 EPS** versus $0.37–0.38 expected, **$12.43 billion revenue** versus $12.2 billion — but the stock fell approximately 10% after CEO Elliott Hill guided Q3 revenue to decline in low single digits with gross margins compressing 175–225 basis points. Gross margin of **40.6%** was already down 300 basis points year-over-year, driven primarily by higher North America tariffs. Net income fell 32% to $792 million.

On the earnings call, Hill described Nike as being "in the middle innings of our comeback" and emphasized his "Win Now" strategy: realigning teams, rebalancing the wholesale–direct portfolio, and investing in sport-led innovation. The running segment grew over **20%** for the second consecutive quarter. Notably, Hill did **not address** the securities litigation, Swoosh governance, or insider trading policy reforms on the call. CFO Friend characterized the results as demonstrating "the resilience of our portfolio" while acknowledging headwinds from "repositioning our business in a dynamic operating environment."

The **March 6, 2026 credit facility renewal** — a $1 billion 364-day unsecured revolver with Bank of America (expandable to $1.5 billion) at SOFR + 59.5 basis points with no financial covenants — replaced the prior year's identical facility with zero outstanding. This is a routine annual renewal supporting commercial paper issuance and working capital. Credit ratings remain stable at **A+ (S&P)** and **A2 (Moody's)**, with no further rating actions since the July and November 2025 downgrades. Multiple bonds mature in 2026, including 2.375% notes due November 1, 2026.

**Q3 FY2026 earnings** are scheduled for **March 31, 2026** at 1:15 PM PT (originally planned earlier but delayed "to accommodate for several holidays"). NKE shares traded at approximately **$53.77–$54.06** as of March 13, within striking distance of the 52-week low of $52.28. The stock has lost more than **70%** from its November 2021 all-time high of $165.10. Barclays upgraded NKE to Overweight on March 11, 2026, stating "the worst may be behind."

## Conclusion

The 73-day audit window reveals a company executing routine compliance — filing Form 4s on time (with one glaring exception), renewing credit facilities, and preparing for quarterly earnings — while fundamental governance deficiencies from the seven-year investigation remain entirely unaddressed. The Swoosh 13D delinquency has crossed from stale to actively violative under the 2024 rule amendments. The Exhibit 19 self-approval loophole persists through a second consecutive 10-K cycle. The SEC has not communicated with Nike in over 14 years despite escalating insider trading complexity, dual-class voting concentration, and an active securities fraud class action. The simultaneous adoption of 10b5-1 plans by the CFO (a named defendant), the Chief Legal Officer (who then immediately sold), and the Chief Innovation Officer during Q2 — after two consecutive quarters of zero plan activity — creates a new forensic thread that warrants close monitoring through Q3 and beyond. Judge Nelson's forthcoming MTD ruling remains the single most consequential near-term catalyst for the entire investigation.
